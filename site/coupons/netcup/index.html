Our two-source eligibility worksheet

A newsletter benefit and a product deal answer different questions. We built this worksheet by comparing the expanded subscription form with the expanded voucher question on the official deals page. The input is the rule stated in each document. The output is a limited document check, not a prediction that checkout will accept a code. No subscription was submitted and no purchase was made.

Read the intersection, then keep the extra restriction

Both reviewed documents exclude domains, VPS ARM, VPS Lite and merchandise. The deals answer additionally excludes hourly based plans. A short newsletter summary therefore cannot establish that an hourly order qualifies. In our worksheet, a plan with an hourly billing basis enters the excluded branch for the deals route even if its name does not contain ARM or Lite. That branch comes from the deals answer, rather than an invented interpretation of the product name.

The reverse is equally important: a plan missing those excluded labels does not automatically enter an approved branch. It enters a needs-confirmation branch because a product page can carry further conditions. This prevents a common logical jump from not explicitly excluded in a summary to guaranteed eligible in an order. A reader can reproduce the distinction by opening the two source links, expanding their relevant controls, and comparing the exclusion lists before selecting a product.

Turn the amount wording into a strict comparison

The official wording requires an order value higher than the voucher amount. For the advertised five-euro welcome voucher, our synthetic boundary check is order value greater than five euros. A hypothetical five-euro order fails that comparison because equality is not greater than. A hypothetical six-euro order passes only the amount comparison. It does not prove product eligibility, tax treatment, a final payable amount or acceptance of an unissued code.

We deliberately keep the hypothetical inputs out of the price table. They are test values chosen to make the boundary visible, not plan quotes or observed invoices. The same worksheet retains the separate one-voucher-per-order rule. It cannot use two hypothetical vouchers to rescue an otherwise excluded product, and it cannot treat a successful amount comparison as permission to combine benefits.

Keep identity, product and time as separate fields

The newsletter form limits registration to once per email address. That identifies a subscription constraint; it does not establish that every existing hosting customer is excluded. We do not silently rewrite an email-registration rule as a new-hosting-account requirement. The correct unresolved field is whether the selected order satisfies its own conditions after the official voucher is issued.

Our record also separates the date of this review from a redemption deadline. The reviewed newsletter statement does not publish a fixed voucher expiry. We leave that deadline unpublished instead of calling the benefit permanent. Likewise, we publish no reusable code string because the reviewed official route asks the visitor to subscribe for a voucher. The worksheet documents what can be checked before that step, and leaves issuance and redemption untested.
